# Anti-AI Writing Style Guide: Eliminating the Signs of Machine Generation

> **Authoritative Source:** Synthesized from the official community standards in [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).
> 
> **Purpose:** To guarantee that all LinkedIn profile copy, headlines, about summaries, experience bullets, and project write-ups read as authentic, human, and executive-level prose. Any text showing machine hallmarks immediately triggers recruiter skepticism and fatigue.

---

## 1. Negative Parallelisms (The #1 Tell of LLM Writing)

Large Language Models habitually generate artificial contrast statements to sound profound. These structures are dead giveaways of AI generation and are strictly banned.

### Banned Formulas:
* ❌ **"Not just X, but also Y"**  
  * *AI Example:* "Not just a data analyst, but a strategic business partner driving operational change."  
  * *Human Fix:* "Strategy & Operations Analyst delivering financial models and cross-functional automation."
* ❌ **"Not X, but Y"**  
  * *AI Example:* "Solving problems not through guesswork, but through rigorous econometric pipelines."  
  * *Human Fix:* "Builds econometric and statistical pipelines to forecast wholesale power volatility."
* ❌ **"Y rather than X"**  
  * *AI Example:* "Focusing on tangible outcomes rather than theoretical models."  
  * *Human Fix:* "Focused on capital recovery modeling and regulatory policy execution."

> **The Rule:** State accomplishments directly and assertively. Never use theatrical negation or false dichotomies.

---

## 2. Banned "AI Vocabulary" Hall of Fame

Certain words and phrases appear in LLM training outputs at statistically anomalous frequencies. Profiles using these words immediately read as machine-generated.

### Strictly Prohibited Words:
* **The "Delve & Tapestry" Cluster:**  
  `delve`, `tapestry`, `beacon`, `testament`, `pivotal`, `spearhead`, `foster`, `underscore`, `interplay`, `crucial`, `nexus`, `catalyst`, `linchpin`, `bedrock`.
* **The "Holistic & Vibrant" Adjectives:**  
  `holistic`, `multifaceted`, `seamless`, `vibrant`, `unwavering`, `enduring`, `meticulous`, `transformative`, `dynamic`, `groundbreaking`, `visionary`.
* **The "Corporate Cliché" Buzzwords:**  
  `driving synergy`, `bridging the gap`, `game-changer`, `thought leader`, `paradigm shift`, `deep dive`, `rich tapestry`, `testament to dedication`.

> **The Rule:** Replace decorative adjectives and over-inflated verbs with concrete actions (`engineered`, `structured`, `modeled`, `negotiated`, `audited`, `benchmarked`).

---

## 3. Avoidance of Simple Copulatives ("Is" / "Are" / "Was")

AI models systematically avoid simple forms of "to be", replacing them with pretentious, dramatic phrasing.

### The Purple Prose Inflation Trap:
* ❌ *"Serves as a testament to our team's delivery..."* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *"Demonstrates..."* or *"Achieved..."*
* ❌ *"Stands as a key milestone in national reform..."* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *"Secured executive approval for..."*
* ❌ *"Acts as a crucial bridge between business and engineering..."* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *"Translates executive strategy into technical specifications..."*
* ❌ *"Marks a pivotal moment in renewable integration..."* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *"Evaluated grid curtailment risk..."*

> **The Rule:** Use direct, active verbs. Do not use decorative verbs ("stands as", "serves as", "marks a") to inflate simple facts.

---

## 4. The Robotic "Rule of Three" (Compulsive Triplets)

LLMs suffer from a rhythmic compulsion to list nouns, adjectives, and verbs in sets of three. When every bullet point or sentence has three components, the prose sounds synthetic and sing-song.

### Examples of AI Triplets:
* ❌ *"Conducted data cleaning, statistical modeling, and executive presentations."*
* ❌ *"Known for a dynamic, curious, and collaborative approach."*
* ❌ *"Optimizing across speed, efficiency, and cost."*

### The Human Fix:
* Break the pattern. Use **two** strong, specific nouns, or an **asymmetrical** phrase, or an explicit metric:
  * ✅ *"Engineered ETL pipelines in R and benchmarked multiple seasonal time-series models."*
  * ✅ *"Focused on capital cost recovery and merchant price cannibalization across 9 policy scenarios."*

---

## 5. Promotional Puffery vs. Neutral Point of View (NPOV)

AI models are trained to be helpful and flattering, which results in promotional brochure copy filled with unearned superlatives. In high-stakes hiring, puffery signals insecurity.

### Prohibited Fluff:
* ❌ *"An esteemed management consultant with renowned expertise in financial transformation."*
* ❌ *"A brilliant track record of driving unprecedented growth."*
* ❌ *"A passionate advocate for sustainability who consistently goes above and beyond."*

### The Objective Standard:
* State the factual record neutrally. Let **hard, verified numbers** establish the candidate's caliber:
  * ✅ *"Ranked 1st in Cohort (Academic Year 2017/18) | Full-tuition merit scholarship recipient."*
  * ✅ *"Structured $186M in greenfield commercial investment proposals across logistics and packaging."*
  * ✅ *"1st Place National Champion out of 100+ university teams in national business case challenge."*

---

## 6. Outline-Like Future Conclusions ("Looking Ahead...")

A dead-giveaway of AI writing is the formulaic, moralizing closing paragraph that summarizes the essay and offers vague optimism about the future.

### The Hallmark Card Conclusion (Banned):
* ❌ *"As the energy transition continues to evolve, the candidate remains dedicated to pushing the boundaries of what is possible, forging a cleaner, more sustainable tomorrow."*
* ❌ *"With a unique blend of domain and analytical skills, the journey ahead promises even greater impact."*

### The Professional Actionable Close (Required):
* Replace philosophical wrap-ups with a crisp, direct, professional **Call to Action (CTA)**:
  * ✅ Target role titles and sectors of interest.
  * ✅ Geography, visa/work authorization parameters, and preferred work mode (hybrid/remote).
  * ✅ Direct links to GitHub, portfolio projects, demo videos, and contact information.

---

## 7. Self-Audit Checklist Before Presenting Any Section

Before outputting any headline, About paragraph, bullet point, or recommendation script, the agent must run this mental filter:

- [ ] Does this text contain *"not just... but"* or *"rather than"*? (If yes, delete and restate directly).
- [ ] Does it contain any banned words (`delve`, `tapestry`, `pivotal`, `beacon`, `testament`, `foster`, `holistic`)? (If yes, replace with an active verb).
- [ ] Does it use inflated verbs (*"serves as"*, *"stands as"* instead of *"is"*, *"achieved"*)?
- [ ] Are sentences trapped in repetitive three-item lists?
- [ ] Does the closing sound like an inspirational essay rather than a business CTA?
- [ ] Would an experienced hiring manager cringe reading this out loud?
